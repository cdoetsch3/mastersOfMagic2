The organising idea

One mountain range does five jobs

A map feels invented when every feature exists to justify one zone. It feels real when a single landform has consequences it never asked for. Almost everything here falls out of the Ironspine, the range running north–south down the middle of the continent.

Consequence 1 A wet west coast

Weather off the western ocean piles against the cliffs and has nowhere to go. That is Stormcliff Coast (Electro), and Galehaven sits in its one sheltered notch.

Consequence 2 A dry eastern interior

What the range wrings out of that weather never reaches the far side. The Kiln Desert (Solar) is a rain shadow — the most ordinary way a real desert forms.

Consequence 3 Passes are the only roads north

Frostfell Pass is not "the ice zone", it is the way through — sea moisture lifted over the crest by steppe wind and frozen there. The road has to use it, so the player does.

Consequence 4 Rivers, and therefore towns

Its eastern flank feeds the River Concord, its southern feeds Glimmerbrook. Concordance sits at the head of navigation; Pennycross where the river road crosses the mountain road.

Consequence 5 The Kinetic tier is a place, not a list

Stone, storm and wind are what a mountain range is. The middle tier needed no invention at all — Old Quarry, Stormcliff and Windward Steppe are three faces of one massif.

The spur The Meridian Scarp

An east–west ridge branching off the Ironspine. Its sunlit south face bakes into the Kiln Desert; its north face never sees direct sun at all. One ridge generates both halves of a counter edge.

The second axis

Latitude is daylight, and daylight is the tier ladder

The gauge down the left of the plate is not decoration. The world's north–south axis is its sun axis, which is why the four tiers stack cleanly onto it and why the Celestial and Ethereal elements land where they do:

Long day · Primal (1–14)

Low, temperate, well-watered. Woods, brooks and foothills — the only part of the world with ordinary weather.

The high frame · Kinetic (15–29)

Not a latitude but an altitude: the tier where the ground itself takes over from the climate.

Low sun · Celestial (30–44)

High, thin, cloudless. The sun never gets far overhead, so the sky is the dominant fact — deserts by day, hard dark by night. Meridian observes from the highest dark-sky ground on the continent.

Polar night · Ethereal (45–60)

Far enough north that the sun stops rising for part of the year. The Umbral Wastes need no magical explanation for the dark, and Hallowmarch reads as exactly what it is — a lit causeway held open against it.

The Sunless Reach was the test case. "Where day never comes" could have been hand-waved anywhere. Sitting it on the shadowed face of the same escarpment that makes the Solar desert means the zone, its counter edge and its neighbour all come from one piece of terrain — and a player can see why just by looking at the map.

Placement rule

Every hybrid lies physically between its parents

The design already says a hybrid zone teaches the matchup of the two elements that meet there. Geography can carry that without a word of exposition — so each one is sited on the actual seam.

Placement rule

Every town sits where two things meet

Towns are never placed for convenience. Each one is at a confluence, a crossing, a harbour, or a pass — the places settlements actually appear.

Open

Three things this raises

Proposal Zenith is not on the plate

It is the highest point of the sky's arc, it teleports to every city, and it is reached only by finishing the Citadel. A city in the sky explains all three at once — and means the final reward is literally leaving the map. The conservative alternative is the summit above the Citadel. Either way it needs a ruling.

Discrepancy There are ten hybrids, not nine

The map section is headed "12 pure zones + 9 hybrids", but its own tables list ten hybrid rows plus the Citadel. Counted while taking inventory for this plate; the header is the part that is wrong.

Unnamed The continent has no name

Nothing in the docs names the landmass, so the plate says "The Known World" rather than invent canon quietly. Candidates that dodge the existing collisions: the Waning Lands, the Twelve Reaches, Sunfall.
