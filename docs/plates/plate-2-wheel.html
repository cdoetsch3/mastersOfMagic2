The discovery this layout is built on

Every tier opens with its radiant element

Reading the counter map straight out of element.dart: Pyro beats Flora, Electro beats Aero, Solar beats Lunar, Sanctus beats Umbra. The element that opens all four triangles is the bright one every time — fire, lightning, sun, holy light. That is not something the docs claim anywhere; it falls out of the data.

So the wheel gives each position in the triangle its own spoke. Counters then rotate the same direction on every ring — the faint arcs — and the three spokes turn out to be three coherent countries rather than three arbitrary slices.

The Radiant March

Pyro · Electro · Solar · Sanctus

Every tier's first element — the one that opens its counter triangle — is the radiant one. Fire, lightning, sun, holy light. The wheel puts them all on one spoke and points it sunward, so the hottest, brightest country is a single wedge of the world.

The Shrouded March

Flora · Aero · Lunar · Umbra

The elements each tier's radiant one beats. Read outward it is canopy, wind, moonlight, then true dark — a spoke that gets quieter and colder the further you walk it, ending in the Umbral Wastes at the rim.

The Deep March

Aqua · Geo · Astral · Arcane

The closers — each beats its tier's radiant element and shuts the triangle. Water, stone, the stars, and the arcane: substance giving way to pattern. The world's only sea lies along this spoke.

What the geometry says for free

Position encodes the kind of place

On a rectangular map a hybrid is just a dot between two other dots. Here it cannot be: the ring carries the tier and the spoke carries the counter position, so where a zone can legally sit already tells you what it is.

Two consequences worth stealing

What this layout gets that a continent cannot

Zenith is the hub

On a wheel, the one point equidistant from every city is the centre — and the centre is Hearthwood. So Zenith becomes the sky directly above the village you started in. You spiral out for fifty levels, break the Citadel at the rim, and the Crown lifts you to a city hanging over your own home. Teleports to everywhere stop needing a justification: it is over everywhere.

Difficulty is radius

"Distance from home" stops being a claim and becomes a measurement. A zone's level band is readable off the plate with a ruler, and any future zone has exactly one honest place to go. The three dashed seams also give the Citadel a spine — it sits at the rim of the same seam the Molten Deep and the Shattered Orrery sit on.

What it costs, plainly. Real climate reasoning mostly goes away. There is no rain shadow, no windward coast, no escarpment casting the Sunless Reach into its own shadow — those were the best parts of Plate I and this layout cannot host them. A wheel is a diagram of the rules; a continent is a diagram of the place. Worth knowing which one you want the player looking at.
