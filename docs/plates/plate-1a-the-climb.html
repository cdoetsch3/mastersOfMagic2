What changed from Plate I

Three consequences of making the finale a climb

The Ethereal tier is now one mountain, not a region

In Plate I the last six zones were spread across a polar plain — six dots in the snow, no relationship between them. Now they are faces and shoulders of a single massif: the Ironspine does not end, it culminates. Every Ethereal zone is defined by where on the mountain it sits, which is a far stronger organising idea than 'far north'.

Zenith is the summit, and the Citadel is in the way

The summit sits above the Citadel on the same ridge, so "you cannot reach Zenith without clearing the Citadel" stops being a rule the game enforces and becomes a fact of the terrain. Teleports to every city need no justification either — it is the one point in the world with line of sight to all of it.

Celestial became the shelf you climb to

Pushing Celestial up to 1 700–2 700 m makes the Kiln Desert better justified, not worse: high-altitude deserts are the sunniest places on Earth because thin air scatters less. Solar magic belongs on a cold high plateau with a brutal sun far more than it belongs in a low hot one.

The mountain does the work

One landform generates the whole Ethereal tier

This is the same trick the Meridian Scarp pulled in Plate I — a sunlit face and a shadowed face off one ridge — but at the scale of a tier. Each Ethereal zone is now a place on the mountain, and two of the three Ethereal counter edges fall out of the geometry rather than being asserted.

Put the consecrated causeway on the warm face and the wastes on the cold one, and bore the Reliquary Deep between them — the counter edge, both its parents, and the hybrid that teaches it are one mountain.

Still open

Four things this revision raises

The massif needs a name

It is now the most important landform in the world and has none. The Vault is my pick — the vault of the sky, and it houses the Reliquary — which makes Zenith the vault's own apex. Others: the Last Rise, Heaven's Stair, the Crown (though that collides with the Concordant Crown).

Two zones now sit off the climb

Tidewrack Shoals is a level 36–40 zone at 20 m, reached by sea from Galehaven rather than by ascent — which hands the port a genuine endgame purpose the docs were looking for. The Molten Deep is the mirror: the one zone that goes down. Worth keeping both as deliberate exceptions.

Gear-versus-altitude is a real design question

The docs already say gear must be worth about ten levels across the L45–60 band. If that band is also a physical climb, altitude is the obvious place to hang it — thin air as a debuff answered by equipment. Or that is a mechanic too many. Either way it should be a decision, not a side effect of the map.

Hallowmarch is no longer a marsh

The name came from a drowned causeway at sea level. On a warm mountain flank it reads as a march in the older sense — a borderland — which still works, and glacial meltwater on a thawing south face is defensible. But if it should stay literally marshy, it wants a different slot.
