What a section shows that a map cannot

Three findings that only appear once you plot height

Altitude and difficulty are different axes

Numbering the places by height instead of by level makes the mismatches impossible to miss. Tidewrack Shoals is a level 36–40 zone sitting at 20 m — lower than Aldermere. Galehaven is a Kinetic port at sea level, while Ashfall Vale is late-Primal content at 700 m. The plan view hides this completely; here it is the first thing you see.

Consequence: any rule of the form "higher means harder" is wrong. Height is a climate input, not a difficulty input.

The tree line is the only hard number in the docs

Rimeholt is described as “the last mortal outpost, above the tree line.” That is the single explicit altitude constraint anywhere in the design, and this is the only projection that can honour it. Put the line at 2 800 m and it does real work: it sorts the Ethereal band from everything else, and it explains why Rimeholt is an outpost rather than a city.

Consequence: Ethereal is not merely far, it is uninhabitable. Vespergate brewing its own potions stops being flavour.

Two zones are underground, and it matters

The Molten Deep sits at −400 m — the only place on the map below sea level, which is exactly right for magma reached through a quarry. The Reliquary Deep is a vault at 2 350 m: underground, yet higher than most of the world's surface. “Sanctity buried in the dark” and “deep” mean two different things, and only a section shows that.

Consequence: these two want interiors, not outdoor art — a different art-production task from the other twenty-nine.

What it costs

A section cannot show east–west and north–south at once. Latitude is collapsed into haze here, so this plate cannot tell you that Frostfell Pass lies between the coast and the steppe, or that the Reliquary Deep sits on the seam between Hallowmarch and the Wastes. It is the wrong drawing for planning routes and the right one for setting art direction, climate, and enemy biome — the atmosphere bands are effectively a lighting brief for each tier.

Read alongside Plate I it is complementary rather than competing: Plate I fixes where, this fixes how high and how lit. The one number here that Plate I should adopt outright is the 2 800 m tree line, because the docs already imply it and nothing else pins it down.
