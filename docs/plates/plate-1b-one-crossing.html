What changed from Plate I

Three consequences of making the finale a climb

Celestial stays on the ground, and that is not a compromise

Concordance is a Celestial-tier town and the trade capital of the world — bank, auction floor, contracts, hour-long buffs. Meridian is an observatory, and you observe the heavens from the world. Neither can sit on a cosmic plane. The zone names agree: Starfall Basin is a crater field of fallen stars, the Mirrormere is a reflection on a lake. Celestial is the heavens touching the ground.

Only Arcane goes above the world

Arcane is the one element with no natural referent. Pyro is fire, Aqua is water, Solar is the sun — Arcane is magic itself, and it has never had terrain to live in. That is exactly why the Collapsed Academy and the Unwritten Library always read as placeholders. Giving Arcane a non-place solves a real problem instead of adding flavour.

The Citadel is the way back IN

Make the last pitch impassable and the whole finale locks into place. You leave the world at Vespergate, cross the Empyrean, and re-enter at the summit through the Citadel. Zenith stays the summit and stays sealed behind the Citadel with no contrivance; Vespergate becomes the last place with a supply line to reality; and Eclipsed finally means something structural — the Citadel is what stands between you and the light at the top.

The mountain does the work

One landform generates the Sanctus–Umbra half of the tier

This is the same trick the Meridian Scarp pulled in Plate I — a sunlit face and a shadowed face off one ridge — but at the scale of a tier. Each Ethereal zone is now a place on the mountain, and the Sanctus ▸ Umbra counter edge falls out of the geometry rather than being asserted.

The Sanctus ▸ Umbra edge is now terrain. A north wall at polar latitude never receives direct sun at any hour of any day, while the south flank is the only side that thaws. Put the consecrated causeway on the warm face and the wastes on the cold one, and bore the Reliquary Deep between them — the counter edge, both its parents, and the hybrid that teaches it are one mountain.

The crossing

You reach the top by going around, not up

Three steps, and each one does a job the others cannot. Out at Vespergate, because the mountain simply stops being climbable. Around through the Empyrean, which is the only stretch of the game with no ground, no weather and no moon. Back in at the summit through the Citadel — so the last boss is not a wall at the end of a corridor, it is the door.

The Empyrean is deliberately small: three places, not a tier. It is a passage with consequences, not a second world to populate — which keeps the art and content cost proportional and stops the finale feeling like it happens somewhere the player has no stake in.

Environment as difficulty

Thin air belongs to Celestial, because Ethereal already has a mechanic

The L45–60 band already runs enemies up to ten levels above you with gear closing the gap. Stacking an environmental debuff on top of that is how you build a wall. Celestial is the empty slot — and putting thin air there makes it a tutorial for Ethereal's whole thesis: the environment hurts you, equipment is the answer. The ten-level gap then reads as a graduation rather than a surprise.

Thin Air · Celestial · 1 700 – 2 700 m

A visible, named status on entering the shelf: you deal reduced damage. It must be asymmetric — it hits you, not the natives, who are acclimatised. Symmetric would be a pure time tax: every fight longer, no outcome changed. Answered by gear and by alchemy.

No moon · The Empyrean

There is no moon above the veil, so Lunar's Full Moon bonus never fires in the final act. Nearly free to build — the phase is already a pure function of the turn number — thematically exact, and it stops you leaning on one element for the finale, which is the Citadel's stated job.

Two deliberate exceptions

Tidewrack Shoals is a level 36–40 zone at 20 m, reached by sea from Galehaven — which finally gives the port an endgame purpose. The Molten Deep is its mirror: the one zone that goes down, at −400 m. Both are kept on purpose rather than forced onto the climb.

The tree line at 2 800 m

The only hard altitude the docs ever implied — Rimeholt is "above the tree line." Pinning it sorts the Ethereal band from everything else and explains why Rimeholt is an outpost and not a city. Hallowmarch is released from being a marsh; march in the older borderland sense fits a thawing south flank.
